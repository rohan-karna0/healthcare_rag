WHO Diabetes Fact Sheet

Diabetes is a chronic, metabolic disease characterized by elevated levels of blood glucose (or blood sugar), which over time leads to serious damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys, and nerves.

The most common is type 2 diabetes, usually in adults, which occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't make enough insulin. Type 1 diabetes, once known as juvenile diabetes or insulin-dependent diabetes, is a chronic condition in which the pancreas produces little or no insulin by itself.

Hyperglycaemia, or raised blood sugar, is a common effect of uncontrolled diabetes and over time leads to serious damage to many of the body's systems. Symptoms include excessive excretion of urine, thirst, constant hunger, weight loss, vision changes, and fatigue.

Prevention of type 2 diabetes can be achieved through regular physical activity, a healthy diet, maintaining normal body weight, and avoiding tobacco use. Early diagnosis and treatment are important to prevent complications.

People with diabetes can live well if they manage their condition with medication, education, and support. Access to affordable treatment, including insulin, is critical.
